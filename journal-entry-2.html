Journal Entry #2

Date: May 14, 2025

A Visit to Hometown

It started like any regular day—I was lounging at home when a friend called to invite me to his wedding. It was sudden, but the kind of surprise that adds color to life. I told him I’d confirm once I got my leave approved.

A few days later, just when I was about to book my tickets, I received another call—this time from my elder brother in my hometown. He told me that our family was planning a trip to Arunachal Pradesh, and as fate would have it, it was scheduled for the same date as the wedding. It became a difficult decision. On one side, a close friend’s big day, and on the other, a rare opportunity for a full family trip—something we’d been putting off for a long time. After some thought and conversations, I chose to go with the family.

I landed back home on the 8th of May and was picked up by my brother. After freshening up, I spent some heartwarming time with my little nephew—his energy is infectious and a genuine escape from everything else.

Later that day, we headed to the Mehendi function for my friend. It was a vibrant evening filled with music, family, and celebration—but as the crowd grew, my anxiety began to creep in. I didn’t know many people there, and they didn’t know me either. That feeling of being the odd one out started to settle in, something I’ve come to recognize too well over time.

Then, something caught my eye—my friend’s cousin sisters had joined the celebration (somehow I know them, thanks). They carried themselves with a sense of ease and confidence that naturally stood out. Well-dressed and composed, they brought a touch of class to the room that didn’t go unnoticed.

I thought about offering a light compliment—something simple and respectful—but, as usual, anxiety got the better of me. It wasn’t a major moment, just one of those small social pauses where you wish you'd spoken up. Still, I remember it with a bit of humor now—just another missed beat in the rhythm of the evening.

As the afternoon wore on and the temperature soared, we gathered in a room to cool down. That’s when another surprise came—I was told I’d be part of the dance performance at the Sangeet function the next day.

With less than 24 hours to prepare, my stress levels went through the roof.

I turned to my brother in disbelief. “How do you expect me to pull this off?” I asked. But he just smiled and told me he believed in me. "You’ll manage, like you always do," he said.

That afternoon, despite the nerves and the heat (yes, even the AC couldn’t keep it cool), we practiced.

Afterwards, we stepped out for Lunch. A plate of momos, even without the mayo I usually prefer, and a chilled Coke brought the day to a comforting close.

The next day—the Sangeet—arrived fast. My anxiety was still high, but I went through with the performance. I danced. It wasn’t about perfection, it was about showing up despite everything trying to hold me back. That meant something.

What followed that evening is difficult to put into words. There were some personal moments that shook me emotionally. It’s something I’ll choose to keep private for now, but it served as a reminder that emotional vulnerability often hides beneath the surface of big smiles and loud music.

On the 10th, my older sister unexpectedly arrived from Delhi. Her presence changed the atmosphere immediately. She has a way of bringing lightness with her—and she never comes empty-handed. I’ve always admired that about her. With her came our youngest cousin as well—lively, mischievous, and a little spoiled, but in the most lovable way. Since he lives in the same house where I was staying in our hometown, he naturally tagged along for the trip. His energy added a playful layer to the group, keeping things light even during long hours on the road.

Our trip to Arunachal Pradesh began that day. We stayed at Maigung Resort in Pasighat, and the place was peaceful, surrounded by nature’s quiet comfort. After the long drive, we had a simple dinner and rested.

The next day brought sightseeing—viewpoints, a waterfall, and a lot of walking under the sun. I got a tan and a fresh layer of sweat, but I didn’t mind. What really stood out was lunch at Moti Mahal Delux—a hidden gem in such a remote place. The food was excellent, and for a moment, everything felt still and right.

May 12th was a slower day. Everyone was busy—my sister with work, my brother with family. And I found myself a little lost in the stillness. The high energy of the last few days had vanished, and the quietness brought with it a strange emptiness.

Finally, May 13th arrived, and I boarded my flight back.

Looking Back:

This trip wasn’t perfect—but it was real. There were high points of laughter, unexpected dance performances, and moments that challenged me to step out of my comfort zone. There were also quieter moments—of doubt, of loneliness, of anxiety. But those, too, are part of the journey.

Going home isn’t always about comfort. Sometimes, it’s about facing your own reflection, surrounded by the people who see you most clearly—even when you can’t.
